# Rights, licensing, and takedown policy

OpenShelf helps readers discover books that are free or openly available. A work being accessible on the internet does not by itself permit copying, hosting, formatting, or redistribution.

## Allowed catalogue entries

The project may link to authoritative source pages. A future hosted-file service should only accept:

1. Work submitted by its rightsholder or a representative authorized to distribute it.
2. Work whose public-domain status has been reasonably checked for the intended territory and type of work.
3. Work under a licence that permits the exact planned use, including sharing and any reformatting or adaptations if applicable.

## Licence checks

Record the exact licence URL and preserve licence notices and attribution. Creative Commons licences differ: noncommercial, share-alike, and no-derivatives terms affect what the service may do. Do not assume every licence permits every use.

For public-domain titles, record the evidence and territory considered. Copyright terms, editions, translations, illustrations, and introductions can have different statuses. A source's US public-domain status does not automatically prove that a work is public domain in every country. This policy is not legal advice.

## Status labels

Use distinct statuses rather than one overall “free” badge:

- **Source listed** — a source link is present; all rights have not necessarily been independently confirmed.
- **Rights reviewed** — a reviewer recorded a licence or rights basis.
- **Format checked** — a specific file passed named structural/quality checks.
- **Kindle transfer tested** — transfer and basic rendering were tested on a named device or supported path.

A title can pass one check and fail another.

## Requirements before hosting files

Before enabling uploads, provide private pending/quarantine storage, a proof-of-rights field, uploader declaration, human review, malware scanning, format validation, file-size/rate limits, abuse reports, a designated contact route, an action log, backups, and a repeat-infringer/appeals process. Never publish an upload automatically.

## Takedown or correction request

Open a GitHub issue titled **Rights/takedown request** without uploading the disputed work. Include the source URL, relevant title/edition, rights basis for the request, and requested action. Avoid sharing private personal data publicly; use a private contact route for confidential communication.

Maintainers should temporarily remove or disable a disputed OpenShelf catalogue link while reviewing a credible report. The current prototype only links to external sources, so OpenShelf cannot remove a file from another website.
