About Great Expectations

Pip, a boy raised in modest circumstances, receives the prospect of becoming a gentleman and begins to imagine a different identity. His new ambitions affect his friendships, loyalties and understanding of what money can and cannot change.

Dickens turns the coming-of-age plot into a critique of snobbery and wishful thinking. Pip’s mistakes make the story about learning to value kindness, gratitude and honest relationships over outward status.
